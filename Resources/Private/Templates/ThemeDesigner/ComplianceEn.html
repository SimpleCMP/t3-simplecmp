§1.1 — Active opt-in, no pre-selection

Valid consent requires an active action by the visitor. Pre-selected checkboxes, "continue browsing" as consent, or ignoring the banner are all insufficient. The CJEU decided this in 2019 with Planet49 (C-673/17) and the German BGH confirmed it in 2020 with "Cookie II" (I ZR 7/16); the EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 and the German DSK guidance (December 2022) draw the same line.

What this means for the configuration:
  • All non-essential services default to "off". In the SimpleCMP service registry, that means no default: true unless the service is genuinely essential and marked required: true accordingly.
  • Closing the banner via the Esc key, browser back button or "X" button must not be counted as consent.

Sources: CJEU Planet49 (01.10.2019), BGH I ZR 7/16 "Cookie II" (28.05.2020), EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent, DSK guidance on telemedia v1.1 (December 2022).

§1.2 — Equal-prominence buttons & WCAG contrast

The accept and reject buttons must not differ visually — same size, same font weight, same background color, same border. Meaning is carried by the label, never by visual weight. The German BGH's "Cookie II" ruling (I ZR 7/16) and the Austrian DSB's ORF.at decision (D124.0507/24, October 2024) drew this line precisely: a "green accept button next to a faded grey reject link" is unlawful.

The text contrast of every button against its background must meet the WCAG AA threshold of at least 4.5:1. The Austrian DSB explicitly cited contrast as a manipulation indicator in the ORF case — a barely-readable reject button works similarly to one that's invisible.

What this means for the configuration:
  • SimpleCMP renders the buttons identically by default — background, border, font weight and padding match across Accept, Reject and Configure. The DOM audit (in the banner above the designer form) verifies this after render.
  • If you override the brand colors, check the accept-text contrast against the accept-background for readability. The DOM audit warns automatically when the value falls below 4.5:1.
  • Custom CSS overrides in the theme module that target only one of the three buttons (e.g. setting "Decline" to grey) break the equal-prominence baseline — please avoid them.

Sources: BGH I ZR 7/16 "Cookie II" (28.05.2020), Austrian DSB D124.0507/24 (28.10.2024), CNIL recommendation on cookie banners (17.09.2020), WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum).

§1.3 — "Reject all" must be available on the first layer

The reject button belongs on the same layer as the accept button — not hidden behind a "Settings" step. The German VG Hannover ruled explicitly in March 2025 (case 10 A 5385/22): a banner with "Accept all" and "Settings", but no "Reject all" on the first layer, is unlawful. A link to a second layer with the reject option is not enough.

The reasoning follows GDPR Art. 7(3) ("as easy as giving consent") and Art. 4(11) ("freely given"). The German Datenschutzkonferenz, the French CNIL and the Dutch AP take the same position; the Austrian DSB decided a similar case against ORF.at (D124.0507/24, October 2024).

What this means for the configuration:
  • The site setting simplecmp.hideDeclineAll stays at false (default). Setting it to true violates established case law.
  • The reject button must be visually equivalent to the accept button — same size, same font weight, comparable contrast. Color softening ("grey link" vs "green button") is also classed as illegitimate manipulation by supervisory authorities.

Sources: VG Hannover 10 A 5385/22 (19.03.2025), Austrian DSB D124.0507/24 (28.10.2024), EDPB Cookie Banner Taskforce Report (January 2023), CNIL cookie-banner recommendation (17.09.2020), Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens — Clear cookie banners (NL).

§1.4 — Granular consent per purpose

Consent must be purpose-specific. Bundling "Analytics + Advertising + Personalisation" into a single checkbox is unlawful; the visitor must be able to decide separately per processing purpose (EDPB Guidelines 05/2020, paragraphs 42 ff.).

In practice: on the second banner layer (settings dialog), one toggle appears per purpose category. "Accept all" may bundle, but only because the visitor has the fine-grained alternative on the second layer.

What this means for the configuration:
  • Every service in the SimpleCMP service registry needs at least one entry in the purposes field.
  • Multiple services sharing a purpose are grouped under that purpose category on the second layer — one toggle per service, shared heading.

Sources: GDPR Art. 6(1)(a), EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent (paragraphs 42–53).

§1.5 — Privacy policy & imprint links

Before the click, the visitor must be able to recognise who is responsible for the data processing and where the full information lives. GDPR Art. 13 requires this. In German, Austrian and Swiss law a second obligation applies: the imprint (TMG, ECG, UWG) must be separately reachable — its own link, not a sub-point of the privacy policy.

What this means for the configuration:
  • simplecmp.privacyPolicyUrl in the site settings points to the site's privacy policy. Without this value, the banner's mandatory link stays empty.
  • simplecmp.imprintUrl points to the imprint. It appears in the banner directly next to the privacy policy.
  • In the SimpleCMP service registry, services that transfer data to third countries (especially the US) should disclose that through their description.

Sources: GDPR Art. 13, TMG (Germany), ECG (Austria), UWG (Switzerland), CJEU C-604/22 IAB Europe (07.03.2024) — obligations for joint controllers.

§1.6 — Persistent revocation trigger

GDPR Art. 7(3) requires: withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it. The visitor must not be forced to create an account or send an email — a visible, clickable affordance in the footer or as a "floating trigger" is sufficient and is what every supervisory authority expects.

What this means for the configuration:
  • simplecmp.floatingTriggerLabel gets a descriptive label ("Cookie settings", "Privacy options"). An empty label effectively disables the trigger — the visitor can no longer find the withdrawal path.
  • The trigger must be reachable from every page, not just the landing. SimpleCMP renders it across the entire site.

Sources: GDPR Art. 7(3), DSK guidance on telemedia (December 2022).

§1.7 — No tracking before consent

§ 25 TDDDG (formerly TTDSG) and Art. 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive are explicit: before consent, neither cookies nor LocalStorage entries may be set, nor may third-party scripts load. Even merely-embedded Google Fonts count as an unlawful US data transfer (LG München I, case 3 O 17493/20). VG Hannover confirmed in 2025 that Google Tag Manager and similar trackers must wait for consent too.

The Dutch supervisory authority levied a €600,000 fine against a pharmacy in 2024 for setting tracking cookies before the banner click — a good indicator for the level of sanction risk.

What this means for the configuration:
  • simplecmp.universalBlocking.enabled must be active. This arms the runtime block for JS-injected scripts and iframes.
  • The server-side HTML rewriter in TYPO3 complements this, gating declarative <script> tags per purpose until consent is granted.

Sources: § 25 TDDDG (14.05.2024), Art. 5(3) ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC, LG München I 3 O 17493/20 (20.01.2022), VG Hannover 10 A 5385/22 (19.03.2025), Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens — pharmacy decision.

§2 — Dark patterns: what is legally forbidden

The EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 (v2.0, February 2023) list six categories of "deceptive design" on digital platforms. While the guidelines nominally target social-media interfaces, supervisory authorities apply the same lens to consent banners. The DSA (Art. 25, in force February 2024) codifies an additional prohibition for very large online platforms.

Skipping — reject harder than accept

Reject-via-link instead of reject-via-button. Reject hidden behind "Settings". Reject only available after manually deselecting every vendor. The "X" in the top corner counted as consent. All explicitly unlawful (VG Hannover 2025).

Stirring — emotional & visual manipulation

Bright-green accept button next to faded-grey reject. Marketing language on accept ("Improve your experience") versus neutral wording on reject. Sad emoji on "Reject". The Austrian DSB ruled in the ORF.at case (2024) that contrast ratios below WCAG AA (3:1) count as manipulation.

Hindering — friction on the privacy-positive path

"Save" requires scrolling through a list. "Are you sure?" confirmation on reject. "Reject all" works only after each category was individually toggled off. All forbidden.

Overloading — exhaustion through too many options

800 vendors in a flat list. 12 purpose categories on the first layer. The fix is progressive disclosure and a maximum of 4–6 purpose categories on layer 1, with vendor lists in expandable accordions.

Fickle — shifting layouts & meanings

Toggle semantics flip between visits ("on = enabled" vs "on = disabled"). The banner looks different on the second visit. Also forbidden — consistency is mandatory.

Left in the Dark — hidden critical information

Third-country transfer hidden away. "Legitimate interest" toggle silently pre-activated. The "essential cookies" category quietly includes analytics trackers too. All unlawful.

SimpleCMP defaults: the banner ships out-of-the-box without these patterns. The buttons render with equal prominence, closing via "X" or Esc does not count as consent, "legitimate interest" toggles cannot be activated, and per-purpose granularity is mandatory in the banner layout. What you should still avoid: your own theme overrides that break button symmetry (e.g. fading the reject-button color in the designer).

Sources: EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 v2.0 on deceptive design patterns (14.02.2023), DSA Art. 25 (Regulation 2022/2065, in force 17.02.2024), Austrian DSB D124.0507/24, VG Hannover 10 A 5385/22.
